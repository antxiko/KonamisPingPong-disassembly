The header

The first sixteen bytes are the header every MSX cartridge carries:

41 42 6C 40 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00
"A" "B"  INIT     STATEMENT  DEVICE   TEXT   reserved

"AB" is the signature, and behind it the address of INIT: 0x406C. The other three entries are zero, so the cartridge adds no BASIC statements and declares no device.

INIT starts no loop

What 0x406C does fits in twenty instructions:

  1. disables interrupts and sets mode 1
  2. writes a jp (0xC3) at 0xFD9A and the address 0x4019 at 0xFD9B, which is the H.KEYI hook
  3. puts the stack at 0xE400
  4. clears the 0x400 bytes of variables from 0xE000
  5. sets the (0xE005) latch, builds the screen, clears it
  6. enables interrupts and sits on jr $

And there it stays forever. The whole game hangs off the interrupt, once per frame, off H.KEYI.

The screen

The eight VDP registers come from the table at 0x47D6, which 0x47C5 pours into R0..R7:

02 E2 0E 7F 07 76 03 E4
regvaluewhat it says
R00x02SCREEN 2
R10xE216K, display on, interrupt on, 16x16 sprites
R20x0ENAME table at 0x3800
R30x7FCOLOUR: base 0x0000, mask 0x03FF
R40x07PATTERNS: base 0x2000, mask 0x03FF
R50x76sprite attributes at 0x3B00
R60x03sprite patterns at 0x1800
R70xE4ink 14 on background 4

R3 and R4 are not addresses: they are a base and a mask. Decoded, they lay out VRAM the other way round from the usual, with COLOUR low and PATTERNS high:

0x0000..0x17FFCOLOUR
0x1800..0x1FFFsprite patterns
0x2000..0x37FFPATTERNS
0x3800..0x3AFFNAMES
0x3B00..0x3B7Fsprite attributes

Reading them as addresses gives the layout swapped, and with it the shapes come out right and the colours in stripes. There is no need to take the reading on trust here: the measured destinations of every compressed block fall inside this layout and not the other, and the cartridge itself confirms it twice by filling 0x0080 with 0xF0 and 0x1600 with 0x70 -values that only make sense as colour-.

R7 is 0xE4 -blue background- while the company logo is up, and 0x4B4B changes it to 0xE0 -black- before painting the player selection.

RAM

The game uses 0x400 bytes from 0xE000, and clears them all at startup. The addresses that come up most:

addresswhat it holds
0xE000 / 0xE001the scene and the step within it
0xE002the mode: bit 5 two players, bit 6 in play
0xE003the frame clock, never reset
0xE004the subscene countdown
0xE005the interrupt reentry latch
0xE008 / 0xE009player 1's control: edges and raw state

0xE070..the sprite attribute buffer, 0x80 bytes

The hidden mark

The last fifteen bytes:

AC B8 9D AC B8 9A 00 98 00 9F 94 89 0C 31 AA

Twelve of title in katakana -コナミのピンポン-, the length 0x0C and the 0x31 of RC-731. Before them sit 70 bytes of 0xFF, whatever was left over of the cartridge.

That mark was discovered by Manuel Pazos, and the finding is his.

Konami's Ping Pong was published by Konami for the MSX in 1985; its catalogue number is RC-731 and it is 16 KB. All rights in the game remain with their holders. This is preservation, study and documentation work, and the cartridge image is not distributed.
